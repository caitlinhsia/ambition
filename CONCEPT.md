# budge.

**the thing you open when you can't get yourself to start**

> most apps reward the people who are *already* motivated. budge is built for everyone else.

A no-login, local-first web app for **any teen who feels stuck or unmotivated** —
that serves one tiny, mood-matched action at a time. Broad enough for everyone;
built well enough to genuinely help the ones struggling with ADHD, depression,
or anxiety — without ever labeling them.

---

## The bet

**Action comes before motivation — not the other way around.**

You wait to *feel like it*, but the feeling only shows up once you've already
started moving. So budge stops asking you to feel motivated. It hands you **one
tiny thing** — something so small that starting it is easier than closing the
tab. Momentum does the rest.

This isn't a hack we made up. It's the mechanism behind **behavioral
activation**, one of the most evidence-backed treatments for low mood —
repackaged as something that feels like a friend, not a clinic.

## Who it's for

Broad on purpose, so nobody has to raise their hand. budge is for **any teen who
feels stuck** — bored, procrastinating, overwhelmed, can't-get-started. Build the
curb cut for the people who need it most, and everyone ends up using it.

Because it's built on real psychology, it quietly holds the ones genuinely
struggling with **ADHD, depression, or anxiety** — without labeling them, and
without anyone thinking *"I'm using the depression app."* A kid dodging homework
and a kid who can't get out of bed open the exact same thing. One just gets
routed somewhere softer, and nobody notices the difference.

## The core loop

```
land → "how are you landing right now?" → get ONE tiny thing
     → do it → tap done → warm, dumb little reward → "another?"
```

That's the whole app. If a teen can't explain it in one sentence, it failed.

## The psychology, doing real work

| principle | what it does |
|---|---|
| **Behavioral activation** | Small actions taken *regardless* of mood break the avoidance → low mood → more avoidance spiral. |
| **Tiny Habits (B=MAP)** | We can't rely on motivation, so we max out *ability*: shrink the task until failure is impossible, and nail the prompt. |
| **Immediate reward** | Emotion wires a habit, not repetition. The payoff lands *the second* you tap done — critical for an ADHD brain that discounts delay. |
| **Self-compassion** | Shame deepens avoidance. Treating an off day as normal is the therapeutic mechanism, not just a nicety. |

## The mood layer

One tap — never a form — quietly re-tunes what comes next. **A low mood is never
met with a demand.**

| you tapped | what's underneath | what budge serves |
|---|---|---|
| 😶 numb / flat | depression, anhedonia | gentlest first steps (open the curtains, drink water). support surfaces more readily here. |
| 😰 wired / anxious | anxiety, avoidance | grounding + a micro-dose of the dreaded thing. approach, too small to panic. |
| 🌀 restless | ADHD-flavored | novel, physical, stimulating. movement, a 2-minute race against the clock. |
| 😴 exhausted | low capacity | permission to rest. one stretch, then back under the covers. rest *is* the win. |
| 🙂 okay | higher capacity | normal tiny wins, plus a gentle offer of something slightly bigger. |

## The rules that make it work (these *are* the product)

1. **One thing at a time.** Never a list. A list is a wall of obligation.
2. **Everything is ≤ 2 minutes.** If it feels big, it's the wrong task.
3. **No streaks, no score you can lose.** Numbers only go up. You cannot fail. Miss a month? Invisible.
4. **Skip is free and guilt-free.** "Not feeling it" deals a gentler card — which makes you *more* likely to do one.
5. **No login, ever.** Start in one tap. An unmotivated teen will not make an account first.

## The philosophy: designed to be outgrown

budge wants to move the skill out of the app and **into your own head** — until
you can shrink any task and start before you feel ready, phone dead, nobody
watching. It teaches that **invisibly, through repetition** — never a lecture.

The scaffolding fades. Leaving is a win state, never a churn event. ADHD,
depression and anxiety aren't things you "complete," so a return after months is
met warmly, not woundedly: *"hey, you're back. no catching up. what's one thing?"*

Which means budge **refuses to measure success by watching you.** No analytics,
no surveillance — just an occasional, opt-in *"how's it going lately?"* on your
terms.

## The guardrails (non-negotiable)

- **Not treatment.** Never claims to treat, never diagnoses, never replaces real care. A nudge, not a clinic.
- **A way to real help, always reachable.** A calm, never-alarming path to actual crisis support. *(Exact form to be finalized before build.)*
- **Radical privacy.** Local-first. Minors' mental-health data lives in the browser, on the device. Nothing leaves.
- **No dark patterns.** No guilt notifications, no loss aversion, no infinite scroll. The manipulative playbook harms this group — so we skip it.

## The voice: the friend who's been there

Warm, dry, lowercase. Never a cheerleader, never a scold. Its warmth shows up as
**not making a big deal of anything** — not your wins, not your absences, not
your bad days.

| moment | line |
|---|---|
| landing | hey, you made it here. rough day, or just a slow one? |
| the task | 10 pushups. bad form absolutely counts. i believe in your bad form. |
| a heavy day | today's a heavy one, and that's allowed. one stretch, then get right back under the covers. that fully counts. |
| done | look at you. that was real, i saw it. |
| you came back | hey, you're back. no notes, no catching up — just good to see you. what's one thing? |
